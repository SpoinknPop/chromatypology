What is Chromatypology?

Chromatypology is a personality and typology system that uses different hues of color to express 12 core motivations, each divided across three Freudian layers of the psyche — the id, ego, and superego — resulting in 36 distinct archetypes.

To find your type, you identify one archetype from each layer, with each coming from a different motivation. The combination of these three archetypes forms your unique Chromatypology profile.

How to type yourself

Select one id, one ego, and one superego archetype — each from a different motivation. You can express your type by color name shorthand or archetype name shorthand.

Example type: Amber · Cyan · Cornflower
Id (Peace): Amber — "The Gourmand"
Ego (Knowledge): Cyan — "The Scholar"
Superego (Expression): Cornflower — "The Creator"

Shorthand: AmbCyaCor  or  GouSchCre

There is also a Disordered category (Black, Gray, White) that sits outside the standard 12 motivations and represents extreme, unhealthy manifestations of personality.

The Three Layers

Chromatypology maps each archetype to one of three Freudian structures of the psyche. Each layer contributes something distinct to your overall personality profile.

Id

Present from birth, the id is unconscious and instinctive — driven purely by pleasure and immediate gratification. In Chromatypology, it reflects our weakest or least healthy behaviors, rooted in egocentric impulses.

Ego

The ego operates in reality. It is the core of our conscious, tangible behavior — balancing the raw id and the idealistic superego through coping and defense mechanisms. The ego is the closest thing to our everyday sense of self.

Superego

The superego is shaped by upbringing, culture, and morality. It provides internalized ideals and social guidelines. Though morally ambitious, it doesn't always align with what is genuinely ethical — that balance falls to the ego.

The 12 Motivations

Motivations are the intrinsic and extrinsic "why" behind our actions and behaviors. Every archetype in Chromatypology is grounded in one of these 12 foundational drives.

  • ControlIndependence · Power · Authority
  • PerfectionLaw · Standards · Morality
  • FameReputation · Popularity · Integration
  • PeaceSerenity · Satisfaction · Diplomacy
  • FreedomPossibility · Liberty · Thrill
  • SuccessAchievements · Victory · Work
  • KnowledgeFacts · Understanding · Curiosity
  • ServiceEmpathy · Support · Positivity
  • ExpressionPassion · Expression · Creation
  • StabilitySecurity · Preparation · Reliability
  • ConnectionPeople · Authenticity · Trust
  • EroticismOptimism · Romance · Idealism
